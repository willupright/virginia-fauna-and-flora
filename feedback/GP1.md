## Strengths (1)

- The goal of project is well defined. This is helpful because it will allow you to build your database more easily with the components of the problem clearly laid out.
- In the introduction, a problem is identified (spread out data about  flora and fauna). This ensures that there will be some amount of interest in the end product.
- A strength of this proposal is the focus on solving a real problem. Information about Virginia’s plants and animals is currently scattered across many places, and the project gives a single platform where data can be stored and shared. This makes the work meaningful because researchers and the public will have a reliable way to learn and make decisions.
- One strength is the topic. I imagine having all of this information in one place would be super helpful to someone who is studying the Virginia ecosystem.
- This project has a clear motivation & purpose. The proposal identifies a real problem: biodiversity data exists but is scattered, making research and learning harder.
- I think the map idea is super cool, and the fact that its split up into different counties is super nice too. It will be great for those who are curious about their own county's flora and fauna.

## Strengths (2)

- There is clear real world relevance. This makes this project not only meaningful for academic growth but also for researchers, educators, etc.
- The team acknowledges that their scope may be too limited, and suggests way to expand it. This is good because it allows and promotes room to grow.
- Another strength is the way the team defined their entities and user groups. By listing species, observations, counties, and users, they show that they understand the core data. This is strong because it gives them a clear foundation for building the database and makes their system design easier to follow.
- Another strength is the abilities of each user. It's nice that the general public can't add things to it, as this information could be incorrect. Allowing the experts to do it allows for the data to stay clean.
- This project has detailed entity & user definitions. Their entities (Species, User, County, Observation) are well chosen and relevant to the queries described.
- Another strength is the admins being able to reject/approve content submitted by researchers. This will help in the case that incorrect information is submitted, or a troll manages to get into the system.

## Improvements (1)

- The introduction correctly points out that virginia has a diverse variety of flora and fauna. It may be to much to tackle in this project. It may be more feasible to tackle only flora or fauna or some other subsections of Virginia's biodiversity.
- While the room to expand if the scope is too limited is a strength, I would say the current scope may already be too large. For example, reducing the scope to only birds or some other category may be desired.
- One improvement would be to limit the scope of the project. They listed many different species groups and also suggested expanding to other states, which may be too much for one semester. Focusing only on Virginia and a few categories of species would make the project more realistic and easier to complete on time.
- One improvement could be to increase the scope of the database to allow for other states as well. Having Virginia as the scope is not necessarily bad, but what happens if I want to check the ecosystems when I travel?
- You could improve the scope. The species list (fish, mammals, arachnids, etc.) is very ambitious. Covering all these categories might make implementation unrealistic.
- One thing they could maybe add that would be cool is if we could see a list of invasive species and what flora and fauna they affect. I think it would be interesting to see, especially considering how much laternflies are spreading across Virginia. They could maybe add it as an attribute like T/F to the species entity.

## Improvements (2)

- Map based visualization, while a really cool idea, may be too difficult to accomplish in the time frame for the project. It will be really important to prioritize core functionality first, CRUD operations, etc., before attempting that problem.
- I think a role table should be created. This would make it easier to give different permissions to different roles (admin, researcher, etc).
- Another improvement would be to separate core features from extra features. The interactive map and quiz tools are creative ideas, but could take more time than expected. Making a smaller set of guaranteed features first will ensure the project is finished and polished before adding larger optional goals.
- One improvement could be data verification. Admins approving the information is good, but would this mean that the admins of the app are Virginia ecosystem professionals? Or do they just run the app?
- The user roles could be refined. “General Public” adding observations might need moderation beyond “maybe admin review.” Clarify how to maintain data integrity.
- I think another way they could improve is adding more detail about the educational activities. How would the quizzes work? Would the Quizlets just be decks or would a mode similar to learn be implemented?
